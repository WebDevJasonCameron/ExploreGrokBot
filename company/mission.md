# Company Mission

## Mission

Build a sustainable, technology-enabled business that creates genuinely useful and enjoyable products while using software, automation, and artificial intelligence to allow a very small organization to accomplish work that traditionally required a much larger company.

The company exists to create long-term financial value for its Owner while protecting the Owner's time, independence, and ability to pursue meaningful creative and technical work.

The objective is not to build the largest possible company.

The objective is to build a **valuable, resilient, and sustainable company**.

---

## Current Stage

The company is currently in the **Exploration Stage**.

No primary market, product, or business model has been selected.

During this stage, the company will:

* Explore problems worth solving.
* Investigate markets and customer groups.
* Experiment with AI-assisted business operations.
* Build reusable research and operating processes.
* Test assumptions before making significant investments.
* Conduct small, inexpensive experiments where appropriate.
* Learn which responsibilities can be effectively delegated to AI agents.
* Develop the Owner's ability to manage an AI-assisted organization.

The company should avoid prematurely committing itself to a particular product, market, technology, or business model.

---

## Long-Term Objective

The long-term objective is to create sufficient sustainable income and owned productive assets to provide the Owner with greater financial independence and the ability to provide long-term security for the Owner's family.

This should be accomplished through the creation and ownership of valuable products, intellectual property, software, systems, content, or other business assets rather than depending exclusively on selling the Owner's time.

The company should increasingly create value that can continue producing benefits without requiring an equivalent increase in the Owner's working hours.

---

## Operating Philosophy

### Stay Small by Design

The company should use technology, automation, AI agents, contractors, services, and carefully designed processes to remain operationally small.

Adding human employees should not be considered a requirement for growth.

Growth should ideally come from increasing the productivity of the company's systems and assets rather than continually increasing headcount.

### Build Assets

Prefer work that creates something with continuing value.

Examples may include:

* Software.
* Games.
* Digital products.
* Intellectual property.
* Data.
* Research.
* Content libraries.
* Brands.
* Automated systems.
* Reusable tools.
* Repeatable business processes.

Not every project must generate recurring revenue, but company effort should generally leave behind something valuable.

### Use AI as Leverage

Artificial intelligence should be treated as productive infrastructure rather than as the company's product by default.

The company does not need to sell "AI products."

Instead, AI should be used where appropriate to increase the amount and quality of work a very small organization can accomplish.

AI agents may perform specialized roles, but the Owner retains strategic and consequential decision-making authority.

### Maintain Human Judgment

AI agents may research, analyze, recommend, create, test, organize, and automate.

They should not determine the company's purpose.

The Owner determines what the company values, what risks are acceptable, what opportunities are worth pursuing, and what ultimately gets created.

### Favor Experiments Over Large Bets

When uncertainty is high, prefer inexpensive experiments that produce information.

Before making a large commitment, ask:

> What is the smallest reasonable experiment that could tell us whether this assumption is wrong?

Failed experiments are acceptable when they produce useful information at an acceptable cost.

### Seek Evidence Before Commitment

Enthusiasm is not evidence.

Market size is not evidence of accessibility.

Complaints are not evidence of willingness to pay.

Technical feasibility is not evidence of business viability.

The company should increase commitment as evidence increases.

---

## Opportunity Philosophy

The company should remain open to opportunities across different markets.

However, attractive opportunities will generally have several of the following characteristics:

* Solve a real and identifiable problem.
* Serve an identifiable customer.
* Have evidence of demand.
* Have evidence that customers spend money in the category.
* Can initially be created by the Owner with assistance from AI agents and software tools.
* Do not require large amounts of outside capital to test.
* Can begin small.
* Have potential to create durable business assets.
* Can benefit meaningfully from automation.
* Do not require the Owner to personally perform every transaction.
* Have reasonable paths to reaching customers.
* Can potentially produce revenue disproportionate to the Owner's ongoing time investment.

These characteristics are guidelines, not requirements.

Detailed evaluation standards belong in `opportunity-criteria.md`.

---

## What the Company Is Not

The company is not being created merely to:

* Chase AI trends.
* Produce generic AI-generated products.
* Follow every apparent market opportunity.
* Maximize the number of projects being worked on.
* Build technology simply because it is interesting.
* Replace human judgment with automation.
* Pursue growth at any cost.
* Create a large organization for the sake of appearing successful.
* Generate short-term revenue at the expense of long-term sustainability.

Technology serves the business.

The business serves the Owner's objectives.

---

## Current Strategic Question

During the Exploration Stage, the company's primary strategic question is:

> **What valuable problems become economically practical for a very small company to solve when software and AI agents dramatically increase the productive capacity of its Owner?**

Research, experiments, and early projects should help answer this question.

---

## Guiding Principles

**Create value before attempting to capture value.**

**Build assets rather than merely creating tasks.**

**Use automation to multiply capability, not complexity.**

**Increase commitment as evidence increases.**

**Remain small enough to remain adaptable.**

**The company is a tool for achieving independence, not an end in itself.**
