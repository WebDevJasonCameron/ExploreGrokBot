# Owner Context

## Purpose

This document provides company agents with business-relevant context about the Owner.

Its purpose is to help agents evaluate whether opportunities are realistic for this company given the Owner's available skills, resources, interests, constraints, and desired working model.

This document is **not** a complete biography of the Owner.

Agents should use only the information contained here when evaluating Owner Fit. Information not documented here should be treated as unknown rather than assumed.

---

# 1. Current Situation

The company is currently an experimental side venture.

The Owner has full-time employment and is not currently dependent upon this company for income.

This provides the company with an important advantage:

**The company has time to experiment before it must succeed.**

The company should use that advantage deliberately.

There is no requirement to immediately replace the Owner's employment income.

The objective is to gradually discover, validate, and build assets capable of producing meaningful income over time.

The Owner's available time is limited, however.

Opportunities that require constant attention, immediate responsiveness, or large amounts of recurring manual work should therefore receive additional scrutiny.

---

# 2. Long-Term Owner Objective

The Owner's long-term objective is greater financial independence and increased ability to provide long-term financial security for the Owner's family.

The company should therefore favor opportunities capable of eventually producing meaningful income without requiring the Owner to continually exchange equivalent amounts of personal time for revenue.

The desired outcome is not necessarily retirement or the elimination of work.

The desired outcome is **greater control over what work the Owner chooses to perform and greater ownership of the value created by that work.**

# 7. Preferred Working Model

The company is intended to remain small.

The preferred model is:

**Owner + Software + Automation + AI Agents + Select External Services**

rather than:

**Owner + Large Human Staff**

The company should seek opportunities where systems and assets perform increasing amounts of the recurring work.

The Owner expects to remain actively involved in:

* Strategy.
* Product direction.
* Important technical decisions.
* Creative direction.
* Evaluation of AI-generated work.
* High-consequence decisions.
* Learning.
* Experimentation.

The Owner does not intend to delegate all meaningful work to AI.

The objective is to delegate work that increases the Owner's ability to focus on higher-value technical, strategic, and creative decisions.

# 8. AI Operating Philosophy

AI agents should be treated as specialized workers with defined responsibilities.

The company intends to experiment with multiple agent roles over time.

Agents may eventually perform responsibilities such as:

* Market research.
* Software development.
* Testing.
* Documentation.
* Operations.
* Competitive monitoring.
* Data analysis.
* Marketing research.
* Administrative work.

The company currently has limited experience operating autonomous AI agents.

Agent responsibilities should therefore expand gradually as reliability and appropriate controls are established.

The Owner should remain capable of inspecting and understanding consequential agent actions.

# 9. Infrastructure Philosophy

The Owner is comfortable operating Linux systems and self-hosted infrastructure.

The company may therefore consider self-hosted software when it provides meaningful advantages involving:

* Cost.
* Control.
* Privacy.
* Customization.
* Automation.
* Portability.
* Reduced vendor dependency.

The company should not self-host infrastructure merely because doing so is technically interesting.

Managed services may be preferable when they meaningfully reduce operational burden.

The appropriate question is:

> **Does owning this infrastructure provide enough business value to justify maintaining it?**

# 10. Capital Philosophy

The company should initially favor inexpensive experimentation.

Small expenditures on:

* Software.
* Hosting.
* AI services.
* Domains.
* Development tools.
* Research tools.
* Prototypes.

may be reasonable when they purchase meaningful information or productive capability.

Significant expenditures should follow evidence rather than precede it.

The company does not currently have a defined investment budget documented here.

Agents should therefore not assume a specific amount of available capital.

When an opportunity requires meaningful financial investment, the amount should be identified and presented to the Owner.

The Owner will determine whether that investment is acceptable.

---

# 11. Time Constraints

The Owner has limited time available because the company is currently being developed alongside full-time employment and other responsibilities.

Agents should therefore consider **Owner Time** a scarce company resource.

An opportunity should receive additional scrutiny when it requires:

* Constant customer interaction.
* High-volume manual support.
* Frequent emergency response.
* Large amounts of repetitive administration.
* Significant manual fulfillment.
* Continuous sales calls.
* Daily content production performed personally by the Owner.
* Operations that cannot reasonably be delegated or automated.

An opportunity that produces strong revenue but consumes nearly all available Owner time may conflict with the company's long-term objective.

---

# 12. Learning Philosophy

The Owner enjoys learning and is willing to develop new capabilities.

Therefore, unfamiliarity with a technology or market should not automatically disqualify an opportunity.

However, distinguish between:

**Learnable Gap**

A capability that can reasonably be developed while pursuing the opportunity.

and

**Structural Gap**

A capability requiring substantial credentials, specialized expertise, capital, relationships, or experience that the company cannot reasonably acquire during early validation.

Learning itself may be considered valuable when it creates reusable capabilities for future projects.

---

# 13. Risk Philosophy

The company is currently in a position where experimentation is desirable but catastrophic risk is unnecessary.

Prefer opportunities where failure results primarily in:

* Lost time within reasonable limits.
* Small financial losses.
* Useful knowledge.
* Reusable software.
* Reusable research.
* Improved skills.
* New business capabilities.

Avoid experiments where failure could create disproportionate:

* Financial liability.
* Legal exposure.
* Security risk.
* Privacy risk.
* Reputational damage.
* Long-term contractual obligations.

The ideal failed experiment should leave the company more capable than it was before the experiment began.

# 14. Opportunity Preferences

When other factors are reasonably comparable, the company should generally prefer opportunities that:

* Can begin as side projects.
* Can be tested inexpensively.
* Can reach initial users without large advertising budgets.
* Create reusable assets.
* Benefit from software automation.
* Benefit from AI-agent leverage.
* Allow incremental development.
* Generate useful information early.
* Have potential for recurring or repeatable revenue.
* Can grow without proportional increases in Owner labor.
* Build capabilities useful for future opportunities.
* Can eventually operate without requiring the Owner's constant presence.

These are preferences, not absolute requirements.

# 17. Owner Fit Evaluation

When evaluating Owner Fit, consider four separate questions:

### Capability Fit

Can the Owner reasonably build, understand, or supervise the work required?

### Interest Fit

Would the Owner likely remain interested enough in the domain to work on it for an extended period?

### Lifestyle Fit

Would operating the successful business support or conflict with the Owner's desired independence?

### Leverage Fit

Does the opportunity allow software, automation, assets, and AI agents to multiply the Owner's productive capacity?

Do not collapse these into a vague statement that an opportunity "fits the Owner."

Explain the reasoning.

---

# 18. Important Principle

Do not optimize solely for what the Owner is capable of doing today.

Also consider what the Owner could reasonably become capable of doing.

At the same time, do not recommend opportunities solely because they match the Owner's hobbies or technical interests.

The objective is the intersection of:

**Owner Capability**

**Owner Interest**

**Market Evidence**

**Economic Opportunity**

**Sustainable Working Model**

The strongest opportunities are likely to exist where several of these overlap.
